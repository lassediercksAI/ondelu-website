How it feels

Real physics.
Real stillness.

A full fluid simulation runs on your iPhone's GPU — the same mathematics that governs real liquids, 120 times per second. It isn't a video loop. Nothing ever repeats.

Stir with your fingers

Each finger drags the fluid like a glass rod. Up to five at once. A quick tap sends out a ripple.

Tilt to catch the light

Turn the phone and the light swings across the flakes, so whole streams flash and fade — just like a bottle of real rheoscopic fluid.

Real inertia

Turn the phone and the fluid lags behind; stop and it runs on, like coffee in a cup. Small wobbles stay small.

Settles into stillness

Put it down and the fluid coasts to a stop, leaving a frozen picture of the last swirl. Thick or runny — you choose the viscosity.

Nothing in the way

No account, no ads, no tracking, no notifications. The controls fade away; it's just the fluid and the glow of your screen.

Honest about power

A living simulation uses the GPU and draws more battery than a still screen. Ondelu is made for short, calm moments.
